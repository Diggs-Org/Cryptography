# Story: The Handler and the Recruit

## Characters

### VANCE
A weathered handler, old enough to remember when tradecraft was taught face-to-face. He is methodical, terse, and speaks in the clipped cadence of someone who has learned that extra words cost lives. He has been in the field, then out of it, then reluctantly back in. He does not explain himself — he tests. His reasons for reaching out to Kay are never stated, but the implication is that he chose her deliberately, and that the choice carries weight.

### KAY
A new recruit, sharp but unbroken. She asks the right questions, though she does not yet know how dangerous the answers can be. She pushes back on Vance's evasions, not out of defiance but out of a need to understand. She has not yet encountered the betrayals that made Vance what he is — and Vance is not sure whether to protect her from that or prepare her for it.

## The Arc

Vance makes first contact. He passes Kay a name and a method, nothing more. As the puzzles progress, small details accumulate — references to a predecessor who is never named, hints that the program Kay joined is not what she was told, and moments where Vance's guard slips and something darker shows through.

There is no conclusion. The arc ends mid-sentence, as field operations often do. What matters is that by the final puzzle, the player understands that Kay is being tested, that Vance is carrying something he has not disclosed, and that the world they are operating in does not reward trust.

## Tone

- Exchanges are short. Both characters communicate like people who know they are being watched.
- Kay's questions land. Vance's non-answers land harder.
- Betrayal is implied, not announced. The darker world shows through gaps, not exposition.
- Neither character is the villain. The system might be.

## Puzzle Conversation Guidelines

Each puzzle exchange follows the same structure:
1. Vance initiates or continues the thread, pushing the mission forward.
2. Kay responds — asking for clarification, pushing back, or revealing something about her situation.
3. A ciphered message appears naturally in the thread (**CIPHER** placeholder until mechanics are wired).
4. Vance provides the minimal hint needed to decode it, in character.
5. Kay's reaction closes the exchange — often skeptical, sometimes unsettled.

The question the player must answer should be clearly answerable by decoding the ciphered message, and should feel like something Kay herself would need to know.
